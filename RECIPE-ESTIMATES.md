## Portion assumptions

- **Chicken Katsu Curry:** 4 portions, each with one breaded chicken cutlet and vegetable curry sauce. The full recipe uses **600 g raw boneless skinless chicken breast, 60 g flour, 2 large eggs (100 g edible), 100 g panko and 40 g assumed absorbed frying oil**, plus one small **92 g S&B Golden Curry Mild box**, potatoes (340 g), onion (150 g), carrot (61 g), 1 tablespoon curry cooking oil (13.5 g), 3 cups water, and **200 g dry medium-grain white rice (50 g per person)**. Salad, the extra egg topping pictured, and discretionary salt/pepper/sugar are excluded.** A larger curry box changes nutrition, cost and water needed.
- **Beef Udon (Niku Udon):** 2 bowls, using **200 g raw shaved beef, 400 g ready-to-use udon, 100 g onion and 5 g oil**. These amounts standardize the unspecified beef/noodle/oil quantities. The original sauce and broth amounts are preserved: 4 tbsp soy sauce total, 3½ tbsp mirin total, 1 tbsp sugar, ½ tsp ginger, 1½ tsp Hondashi and 4 cups water. The complete broth and sauce are included; **optional eggs and green onion garnish are excluded**. Ready-to-use udon weights are not dry-noodle weights.
- Timings (50 and 30 minutes) are estimates. Noodle package cooking times take precedence over the handwritten approximate 5 minutes.

Nutrition uses manufacturer labels for curry roux, panko, noodles, beef, soy sauce and Hondashi; USDA FoodData Central covers dry rice, chicken breast, flour, eggs, vegetables, oil, sugar, ginger, and the branded mirin entry. USDA records were retrieved directly through the public FoodData Central API. Calculations sum whole-recipe quantities, then divide by servings. Calories round to whole kcal and other macros to 0.1 g only after summing. Whole-recipe macro displays scale rounded per-serving figures and may differ slightly from the unrounded ingredient sum.

S&B calls 1/5 of a box one label serving (18 g, rounded); the whole 92 g box is calculated as five label servings, not 92 ÷ 18 servings. Hondashi assumes 3 g per teaspoon; its listed fat range is represented by the midpoint. Mirin calories use the label, with no assumed alcohol evaporation; alcohol and label rounding mean calories need not equal exactly 4×protein + 4×carbs + 9×fat. All quantities are edible ingredient amounts; cooking water has no macros or ingredient cost. For katsu, all coating ingredients prepared are counted, even though some may be left over. Absorbed frying oil is modeled at 10 g per cutlet; this is an assumption, not a measured value. An additional 5 g oil per cutlet adds roughly 44 kcal and 5 g fat. Frying-oil cost covers the 40 g absorbed, not all oil filling the pot: initial shopping cost may therefore be higher, and actual cost depends on whether leftover oil is reused or discarded.

If all udon broth is consumed, its ingredients contribute roughly **2,700 mg sodium per bowl**: mostly the 4 tbsp soy sauce and Hondashi, plus noodles and beef. The amount consumed changes if broth is left behind. This is an estimate, not a sodium measurement.

## Optional egg

One large egg (50 g edible weight) adds approximately **72 kcal, 6.3 g protein, 0.4 g carbs and 4.8 g fat**, based on [USDA raw whole-egg composition](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/171287/nutrients). At the referenced [Walmart dozen price of $1.67](https://www.walmart.com/c/kp/eggs-12-count-great-value), one egg costs about **$0.14**. One optional egg per udon bowl therefore brings it to roughly **702 kcal, 38.9 g protein and $4.95 per bowl**, before optional green onions.
